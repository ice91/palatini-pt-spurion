\[
 (\Box_g-m_\psi^2)\psi=0,\quad

The last equation follows from the derived action (12) and δS_ψ=(1/2)∫√−g T^{μν}δg_μν, so neither its coefficient nor its sign is normalized away. Direct differentiation gives

\[

It is compatible with the Bianchi identity. At ē=dx,ψ̄=0, the free matter and gravity equations decouple at linear order, while stress is quadratic in ψ and the first metric/matter vertex is hψ². \[

This demonstrates one consistent stress coupling, **not universality across all possible matter**. A rule coupling every species to the same reconstructed metric is an additional matter prescription. ## 5. Weinberg–Witten applicability, without a claimed evasion

The energy-momentum part of Weinberg–Witten assumes a Lorentz-covariant conserved local stress tensor whose physical one-particle matrix elements carry the total four-momentum, and excludes massless spin>1 particles carrying that momentum. Gauge covariance of such matrix elements is essential. We neither reprove nor circumvent the theorem.

The BF/Plebanski theory is a diffeomorphism gauge theory. In its GR branch there is no local gauge-invariant Lorentz-covariant gravitational stress operator of the required type carrying graviton energy. Asymptotic energy is a boundary charge; a local gravitational pseudotensor or the gauge-fixed positive reduced Hamiltonian is not that covariant operator. A matter stress tensor alone is not the total stress carrying gravitational-wave energy. Hence the theorem's hypotheses are not all present, just as in ordinary GR; changing terminology to BF or PTQ is not the reason. No flat-background QFT composite-graviton construction satisfying all those hypotheses has been exhibited. See the primary [Weinberg–Witten paper](https://hep.physics.uoc.gr/Erasmus-IP-2013/files/lectures/chiral-symmetry/Weinberg-Witten.pdf), Theorem 2, printed p.60. This applicability comparison supplies no microscopic origin for the assumed gauge geometry.

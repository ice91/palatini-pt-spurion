## 3. Relative entropy and positivity without a wedge trace

For normalized faithful normal states ρ,σ on one fixed algebra, take standard vector representatives ξ_ρ,ξ_σ and the closed relative Tomita operator

\[
 S_{\sigma|\rho}(A\xi_\rho)=A^\dagger\xi_\sigma,\quad
 \Delta_{\sigma|\rho}=S_{\sigma|\rho}^\dagger S_{\sigma|\rho},
 \quad D(\rho\Vert\sigma)=-\langle\xi_\rho,
                  \log\Delta_{\sigma|\rho}\xi_\rho\rangle.     \tag{6}
\]

This fixes our ordering convention explicitly. A finite answer requires the corresponding logarithmic spectral integrability; in general D may be +∞. The Araki construction is existing mathematical input. Its positivity can be reproduced on this domain by spectral calculus: log z≤z−1 and

\[
 \langle\xi_\rho,\Delta_{\sigma|\rho}\xi_\rho\rangle
 =\Vert S_{\sigma|\rho}\xi_\rho\Vert^2
 =\Vert\xi_\sigma\Vert^2=1
 \ \Longrightarrow\ D\geq 1-1=0.                            \tag{7}
\]

The expectation of Δ here is a quadratic-form expectation, sufficient for the inequality; no bounded-operator assumption is made. Spectral regularization yields the extended +∞ case. D=0 precisely when the two normal states agree on the algebra. Monotonicity under restricting the algebra is an established further theorem; a concrete instance is computed below.

For literal faithful finite matrices σ, diagonalize ρ with eigenvalues r_i and σ with s_j. With w_ij=|〈r_i|s_j〉|², the scalar inequality r log(r/s)≥r−s gives independently

\[
 D=\sum_{ij}w_{ij}r_i\log(r_i/s_j)
 \geq\sum_{ij}w_{ij}(r_i-s_j)=0.                            \tag{8}
\]

There is no claim that computing a few positive matrices proves (7) in continuum QFT.

## 4. A local coherent preparation and an actual cocycle calculation

Choose real f∈C_c^∞((d−L,d+L)), d>L>0, and set

\[
 U_\lambda=\exp[-i\lambda\int f(x)\pi(0,x)dx]\in M,\qquad
 \omega_\lambda(A)=\omega_0(U_\lambda^\dagger A U_\lambda).
                                                               \tag{9}
\]

Fixed-time Weyl localization follows from the current net/time-slice construction; the support lies strictly inside the wedge. Smooth compact data have finite one-particle norm and boost-energy form. U_λΩ remains cyclic separating, so both restricted states are faithful. Canonical commutators give

\[
 U_\lambda^\dagger\phi U_\lambda=\phi+\lambda f,
 \quad U_\lambda^\dagger\pi U_\lambda=\pi,\qquad
 \varphi_\lambda(t,x)=\frac\lambda2[f(x-t)+f(x+t)].             \tag{10}
\]

Thus □φ_cl=0 is solved, not a prescribed torsion/background branch. Vacuum means vanish and normal ordering yields

\[
 \Delta\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle=T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm cl}[\varphi_\lambda],
 \quad \Delta\langle T_{00}(0,x)\rangle=\frac{\lambda^2}{2}f'(x)^2.
                                                               \tag{11}
\]

The linear operator term λf'∂_xφ in the shifted quadratic density has zero vacuum expectation. This derives the stress data; no desired information–energy relation was imposed in the action.

For an inner unitary of M in the differentiability domain, relative modular theory gives the computable identity

\[
 D_R(\omega_\lambda\Vert\omega_0)
 =i\left.\frac d{ds}\omega_0[U_\lambda^\dagger
                         \sigma_s^{(0)}(U_\lambda)]\right|_0. \tag{12}
\]

Here its right-hand side can be calculated explicitly rather than inferred from subtracting divergent entropies. Write a Weyl displacement by classical Cauchy data v=(q,p), with

\[
 W(v)=e^{i\int(p\phi-q\pi)dx},\quad
 \Omega(v,w)=\int(qp_w-pq_w)dx,\quad
 W(v)W(w)=e^{-i\Omega(v,w)/2}W(v+w).                         \tag{13}
\]

For (9), v=(λf,0). Under σ_s(W(v))=W(v_s), the classical mean transforms by the inverse of the field boost: φ_cl(Λ_{+2πs}(t,x)). Differentiating this actual solution at t=s=0 gives

\[
 \dot q_s|_0=0,\qquad \dot p_s|_0=2\pi\lambda\partial_x(xf'),
 \quad \Omega(v,\dot v_s)|_0
 =2\pi\lambda^2\int f(x)(xf')'dx
 =-2\pi\lambda^2\int x f'^2 dx.                            \tag{14}
\]

The last step uses the actual compact-support boundary conditions. The Gaussian vacuum characteristic functional gives

\[
 \omega_0[W(-v)W(v_s)]
 =e^{i\Omega(v,v_s)/2}\,
   e^{-\Vert h(v_s-v)\Vert^2/2}.                            \tag{15}
\]

Here h fixes the one-particle normalization so that this is the vacuum Weyl expectation. The real norm term starts at s²; its first derivative is zero. Equations (12)–(15) therefore give

\[
 \boxed{D_R=\pi\lambda^2\int_0^\infty x f'^2dx
              =\Delta\langle K_R\rangle\geq0}.              \tag{16}
\]

The same result is 〈U_λΩ,𝒦_0U_λΩ〉: the left contribution is unchanged because U_λ commutes with the left algebra. The symplectic calculation explicitly checks its sign and normalization, including the opposite signs of modular flow on fields and the induced classical displacement data. A separate numerical derivative/integration of (14) agrees with the stress integral within 10⁻⁴⁰ in the stated illustration.

This is the local-unitary coherent case; no formula for all arbitrary wedge excitations or arbitrary improved stress tensors is claimed. In a factorized regulator U_R changes no eigenvalues of ρ_R, so ΔS_R=0 exactly while ΔK_R=D_R>0. In continuum the direct statement is (16), not a finite absolute S_R. A compatible local-unitary entropy-difference regulator has ΔS_R=0, but a universal finite entropy subtraction was not needed.

## 5. A compact packet, information weighting and accessibility

Fix a profile rather than a free constitutive function:

\[
 b(y)=\begin{cases}e^{-1/(1-y^2)},&|y|<1,\\0,&|y|\geq1,
 \end{cases}\quad f(x)=b((x-d)/L),\quad

\]

Since b'² is even, an exact change of variable gives

\[
 E_{\rm cl}=\frac{\lambda^2 I_b}{2L},\qquad
 \boxed{D_R=2\pi dE_{\rm cl}}.                              \tag{18}
\]

At λ=1/5,L=1,d=3, independent quadratures give I_b≈0.40958706075, E_cl≈0.0081917412151 and D_R≈0.15441068413 nats. These are dimensionless verification illustrations in chosen length units, not fit parameters or empirical data. Formula (18), not the sample numbers, is the result.

For the nested wedge W_b={x−b>|t|}, 0<b<d−L, the same excitation remains entirely inside. Translation covariance repeats (12)–(16) with weight x−b:

\[
 D_{W_b}=2\pi(d-b)E_{\rm cl}<D_{W_0},\quad
 \frac{dD_{W_b}}{db}=-2\pi E_{\rm cl}.                       \tag{19}

Thus losing part of the accessible algebra reduces this state's distinguishability, in exact agreement with monotonicity. This is a change of access/reference modular weight, not entropy production in time. At fixed E_cl, translating the packet to different d changes D_R; ordinary total energy alone cannot determine observer-relative information. No formula beyond the stated packet-containment domain is extrapolated.

## 6. Entanglement first law: nonzero slope and finite correction

For a finite faithful reference σ and normalized differentiable ρ_ε=σ+εX+O(ε²), Tr X=0, **hold the algebra and K_0=−logσ fixed**. Differentiating Tr ρ logρ via the log resolvent gives Tr σ(d logρ/dε)|_0=Tr X. Hence

\[
 \delta S=-\operatorname{Tr}X(\log\sigma+I)
          =\operatorname{Tr}XK_0=\delta\langle K_0\rangle,
 \quad D(\rho_\epsilon\Vert\sigma)
       =\Delta\langle K_0\rangle-\Delta S\geq0.              \tag{20}
\]

The first equality is first order; the second identity is exact on the matrix domain. The positive Hessian is

\[
 D''(0)=\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Tr}
       [X(\sigma+t)^{-1}X(\sigma+t)^{-1}]dt\geq0.             \tag{21}
\]

For an explicitly computed example use I01's d=3 boost-mode regulator q=1/2, not a new field theory:

\[
 \sigma=\frac1{21}\operatorname{diag}(16,4,1),\quad
 K_0=\log(21/16)I+\log4\,N,\quad
 X=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&0\\1&1&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \rho_\epsilon=\sigma+\epsilon X.                           \tag{22}
\]

The sufficient domain |ε|<1/20 is strictly positive by diagonal dominance. X does not commute with σ. Its eigenvalues are

\[
 r_\pm=10/21\pm\sqrt{(2/7-\epsilon)^2+\epsilon^2},\quad r_3=1/21.
                                                               \tag{23}
\]

Differentiating their entropy, independently of the log-resolvent calculation, gives

\[
 S'(0)=\log4=\langle K_0\rangle'(0),\quad
 D(\rho_\epsilon\Vert\sigma)=
 [105/32+(7/4)\log4]\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^3).                \tag{24}
\]

The quadratic term is nonzero: finite ΔS≠ΔK in general. Independent eigensolvers recover D''(0)=11.4145302639… and the common first derivative 1.3862943611…. Number-only measurement dephases X; the quadratic coefficient becomes 105/32, and its relative entropy is smaller. This is a finite operational example of information lost by discarding coherences, not a statement that a single detector measures the entire algebraic D.

The actual continuum coherent path has δS_R=δK_R=0 to first order in λ and D_R=O(λ²). That does not invalidate the first law. The nonzero slope example (22) is a literal regulated calculation; we do not assert that its arbitrary matrix perturbation supplies a smooth continuum stress profile or new gravitational equation.

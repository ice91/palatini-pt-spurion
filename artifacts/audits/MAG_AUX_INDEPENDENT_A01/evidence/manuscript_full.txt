                                                    Abstract
           Independent affine connections are routinely eliminated algebraically in Palatini and
       metric-affine gravity, but projective gauge degeneracy makes it nontrivial to determine
       whether elimination defines a unique reduced theory. A local Hessian criterion separates
       gauge freedom from invariant auxiliary degeneracy.  In a four-dimensional trace/vector
       model with the compensator Aµ varied, the full pointwise one-form-fibre Hessian kernel
       equals the projective gauge image precisely on the regular locus f1 ̸= 0, f2 ̸= 0.  The
       horizontalsolutionis unique, theaffinesolutionsformonelocalorbit, andquotient-firstand
       eliminate-first reductions agree as local bulk functionals modulo the admitted divergence
       currents. At f1 = 0 or f2 = 0, rank loss is not merely enlargement of projective gauge
       freedom: source compatibility may fail, while compatible branches retain gauge-invariant
       algebraic moduli. These statements are local to the stated trace/vector configuration space.

1   Introduction

When may a gauge-degenerate auxiliary field be eliminated before or after taking the gauge
quotient without changing the reduced theory?
    An independent affine connection in Palatini and metric-affine gravity is typically auxiliary,
and the Palatini curvature scalar is invariant under a consistent projective shift [1–6]. Neither
that projective invariance nor algebraic connection elimination is claimed as new [7–12]. What
is not automatic is that the two operations agree.
    Gaugenonuniquenessandauxiliarynonuniquenessneednotcoincide. Rawaffinenonunique-
ness splits into two distinct effects.  Some directions are gauge:  they never enter the bulk
density and are removed by the projective quotient. Other directions are gauge-invariant and
may nevertheless remain auxiliary.  Gauge invariance is not dynamical independence, and
gauge redundancy is not the same as auxiliary eliminability. Only after those two effects are
separated is auxiliary elimination guaranteed to define a reduced spectator theory rather than
a representative choice among invariant degeneracies.
    IndependentaffineconnectionsarecommonlyeliminatedalgebraicallyinPalatiniandmetric-
affine analyses [4, 5, 7–11, 13]. If the affine Hessian has gauge-invariant null directions beyond
the projective orbit, that substitution does not define a unique quotient theory: unresolved
invariant data remain.  The exact kernel criterion is therefore a local diagnostic of when
integrating out the connection is a quotient-independent operation rather than an implicit
non-gauge branch restriction. The diagnostic is established here in the stated trace/vector
sector; it is not claimed for unrestricted metric-affine gravity.
    The trace/vector sector is a minimal nontrivial setting in this model where a projective
one-form orbit, gauge-invariant horizontal affine data, and rank-changing algebraic connection
equationscoexist. Itisthereforeanexactlaboratoryforseparatingprojectivegaugedegeneracy
from invariant failure of auxiliary eliminability. This defined configuration space is not asserted
to be a dynamically consistent truncation of unrestricted 64-component metric-affine gravity.

    On the regular locus of the model below, the vertical projective direction is quotiented and
the horizontal affine variables are uniquely eliminated. Beyond it, extra gauge-invariant kernel
can leave unresolved invariant data rather than additional projective freedom. The algebraic
source conditions are sharp: S2 (f1 =0, f2̸=0) requires ∂f1 =0; S3 (f1̸=0, f2 =0) requires
∂f3 = 0; and S4 (f1 =f2 = 0) requires both. Incompatible sources give no stationary affine
solution, while compatible branches can retain invariant moduli. These statements classify
algebraic branches, not dynamical crossings.
    The existence of a projectively invariant residue is not new here.  A Palatini torsion
construction realized projective invariance through a scalar spurion ϵ, held fixed at the stated
order, with invariant combination Tµ−∂   µϵ [14]. A subsequent operator-basis analysis used an
independentone-formAµ asanondynamicalreferenceontheprojectivefibre,againwithresidue
Tµ−A   µ, without imposing δS/δA [15].  The present paper addresses a distinct variational
and reduction question:  Aµ is included in the auxiliary variational system; the full affine
Hessian is computed and its kernel is compared with the projective tangent; local orbit
uniqueness is established on the regular locus; and quotient-first and eliminate-first reductions
are compared explicitly. Varying Aµ is not proposed as the unique physical completion of the
earlier nondynamical construction; it defines the distinct auxiliary variational problem studied
here. Inthatproblem,full-Hessianrank,sourcecompatibility,orbituniqueness,andelimination
order are tested together.  That a strict projection need not agree with a Schur/Feshbach
reduction is a general methodological point [16]; it is used only as a reason not to assume that
reduction order is innocuous, not as metric-affine evidence.
    Section 2 records a finite-dimensional organizing criterion for quadratic auxiliary densities.
It is a reduction lemma, not a deep new general mathematical theorem. Sections 3–6 supply
the principal scientific content: a nontrivial verification of its hypotheses in a four-dimensional
metric-affine trace/vector model. The Palatini kinetic density 3(∂f1)2/(2f1) is generic struc-
ture [4, 7, 8]. In this varied-Aµ system, the model-specific contribution is the full affine Hessian
and its exact full-fibre gauge kernel, the sharp regular-locus local orbit, explicit equality of
quotient-first and eliminate-first reductions, and singular-stratum source compatibility. The
transverse determinant−f     21f2/6 supplies the regularity test for those results.

9.2   Fingerprint∆  2 = ∆3 = 0

family, not a claim about all conceivable symmetry-compatible operators.

Projective Palatini gravity and Ricci-based MAG connection solutions are known [2, 3, 6, 9, 23].

truncation of unrestricted 64-component MAG [1, 19].

10   Conclusion

Gauge nonuniqueness and auxiliary nonuniqueness need not coincide. For a local quadratic
auxiliarydensity,invariancealongagaugedistributiontogetherwithkerH    =imG    isasufficient
finite-dimensional reduction criterion: it yields a unique reduced solve, one affine gauge orbit,
and equality of quotient-first and eliminate-first on-shell functionals. That organizing lemma
is not the principal claim. In the metric-affine trace/vector model (3.7), the curvature and
compensator targets select (4.1) within the adopted two-parameter family. On f1 ̸= 0 and
f2̸=0 the species determinant is detH    ⊥ =−f    21f2/6, so the pointwise one-form-fibre kernel is
exactly the projective gauge image. Affine solutions form one local orbit, QE=EQ holds as
spacetime-local bulk functionals modulo the displayed currents J1 and J3, and the reduced
functional is (7.1) with the standard Palatini kinetic term 3(f   ′1)2/(2f      1).
    On S2 (f1 =0, f2̸=0), stationary affine solutions require ∂f1 =0; on S3 (f1̸=0, f2 =0),
they require ∂f3 = 0; and on S4 (f1 = f2 = 0), both conditions are required.  Rank loss
can thus mean source incompatibility or gauge-invariant algebraic moduli, not merely extra
projective gauge freedom. These conclusions are local to the trace/vector configuration space;

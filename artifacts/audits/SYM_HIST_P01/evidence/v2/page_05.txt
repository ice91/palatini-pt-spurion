                   Physical Motivation for the Scalar PT Projector
                         We restrict observable scalar densities to the PT-even, real sector for two reasons: (i) on oriented
                   (3+1)-manifolds the combined PT preserves orientation and commutes with the Hodge dual∗, so the
                   Levi–Civita densityϵµνρσ (weight+1) is PT-even; (ii)by theanti-linearity of T, expectationvalues of
                   PT-odd scalar densities are purely imaginary and thus unobservable as real densities. We therefore
                   averagea scalarwith its PT imageand takethe realpart,Equation (8)   . This isa postureon observables,
                   not on fields. The commutation Lemma1and a minimal counterexample with              δϵ ̸= 0 render the
                   posture directly checkable in our setup.

                   2.1. Projective Symmetry and the Stueckelberg Spurion
                         Palatini geometry admits aprojectivesymmetry under which the connection shifts as

                                       Γαµν → Γαµν+δαµξν,        Tµ≡ Tαµα →  Tµ+(d−1)ξµ,       (d=4).                        (5)

                   To maintain invariance of the PT-even observable sector after imposing the scalar projector, we
                   introduce a Stueckelberg compensatorϵ whose gradient transforms as

                                                                  ∂µϵ → ∂µϵ+(d−1)ξµ,                                                               (6)

                   and we work with the projectively invariant trace combination

                                                       Tµ ≡  Tµ−∂µϵ            (projectively invariant).                                             (7)

                   Within the two–derivative truncation adopted here, we take the spurion limit in which dynamical
                   fluctuations of ϵ are frozen; thus the allowed PT-even scalar sector depends on ∂ϵ only through
                   Tµ. Combined withA1–A5,thisensures thatthescalarprojector commuteswithvariationonscalar
                   densities (Lemma1). In Section3we enumerate the resulting quadratic basis, and in Sections4–7we
                   show how (C1) aligns Tµ with∂µϵ so thatTµ→ 0 at the level analyzed.
